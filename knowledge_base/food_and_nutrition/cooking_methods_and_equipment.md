# KeepReady — Emergency Cooking Methods, Thermal Physics & Fuel Engineering

This manual covers off-grid meal preparation, thermal energy retention, cold-soaking protocols, manual grain milling, and fuel conservation when gas and electricity infrastructure fail.

---

## 1. Thermal Retention Cooking (The Haybox / Wonderbag Technique)

In a protracted emergency, fuel (gas, wood, charcoal) is as finite as ammunition. The thermal retention method cuts cooking fuel consumption by **75% to 80%**.

### The Physics:
* Food does not need active flame to cook; it needs prolonged exposure to temperatures above **82°C (180°F)** to soften starches and denature proteins.
* Standard simmering wastes 80% of heat radiation into the ambient air.
* An insulated thermal cooker captures the food's sensible heat mass and maintains pasteurization and cooking temperatures for hours.

### Operational Haybox Protocol:
1. Bring water, grains, beans, and seasonings to a vigorous rolling boil in a heavy-bottomed, tightly lidded pot for 5 to 10 minutes over flame. Ensure internal food temperature reaches 100°C.
2. Immediately remove the sealed pot from heat.
3. Place inside an insulated chamber (a thermal insulated cooking bag, a rigid camping cooler packed with wool blankets, or a box packed with clean straw).
4. Seal insulation securely. Do NOT open the lid to inspect (each opening loses 5°C to 10°C of heat).
5. Let sit for the designated thermal cooking time.

### Thermal Retention Cooking Timetable:
* White Rice (Polished): Boil 3 minutes -> Insulate 1.5 hours.
* Rolled Oats: Boil 2 minutes -> Insulate 45 minutes.
* Brown Lentils: Boil 5 minutes -> Insulate 2 hours.
* Soaked Pinto / Black Beans: Boil 15 minutes -> Insulate 4 to 6 hours.
* Vegetable & Canned Meat Stew: Boil 10 minutes -> Insulate 3 to 4 hours.
* Critical Food Safety Rule: Consume food while still hot. If pot temperature drops below 60°C (140°F) for more than 2 hours, reheat to a full boil before eating.

## 2. Stovetop Pressure Cookers (The Off-Grid Workhorse)

A traditional non-electric stovetop pressure cooker (working at 15 PSI / 121°C) is the most valuable cooking tool in a bug-in inventory:
* Fuel Reduction: Cuts active boiling time by 70%.
* Water Conservation: Steam cannot escape the weighted valve, reducing water loss to less than 5% compared to open-pot evaporation.
* Aged Bean Softening: Overcomes the hard-seed coat of aged stockpiled beans in 20 minutes instead of 90 minutes.

## 3. The Cold-Soaking Methodology (Zero-Fuel Preparation)

When fire, smoke, cooking odors, or lack of fuel forbid lighting a stove, certain emergency rations can be rehydrated at ambient water temperatures:

### Tested Cold-Soaking Timetable (Water at 20°C / 68°F):
* Instant Mashed Potato Flakes: 1 minute (Instant rehydration).
* Rolled Oats: 20 to 25 minutes.
* Couscous (Pre-steamed semolina): 8 to 12 minutes.
* Ramen Noodles (Instant fried noodles): 20 to 30 minutes.
* Freeze-Dried Meals & Vegetables: 15 to 25 minutes.
* Textured Vegetable Protein (TVP - small mince): 15 to 20 minutes.
* Minute / Parboiled Rice: 15 to 20 minutes.
* Beef Jerky: 30 to 45 minutes (softens into chewable meat fibers).
* Dehydrated Refried Beans: 10 to 15 minutes.
* What CANNOT be cold-soaked: Raw white rice, raw hard wheat, raw dry unsoaked beans, raw pasta (remains tough, indigestible starch).

## 4. Manual Grain Mills (Wheat-to-Flour Engineering)

Whole wheat berries store for 30+ years, but are inedible as bread without mechanical grinding.

### Burr Types:
* Hardened Steel Burrs:
  - Pros: Unbreakable, can grind oily commodities (corn, soybeans, peanuts into butter, coffee beans) without clogging.
  - Cons: Produces a slightly coarser flour; requires more turns per cup.
* Corundum / Ceramic Stone Burrs:
  - Pros: Produces the finest, bakery-grade pastry flour in a single pass.
  - Cons: Cannot grind oily grains or seeds (fats glaze the stone pores permanently); stones can chip if stones/debris enter the hopper.
* Survival Recommendation: A dual-burr manual mill with interchangeable steel and stone heads.
