# KeepReady — Long-Term Potable Water Storage, Container Engineering & Reserve Harvesting

This manual details the empirical engineering, container selection, biological stabilization, and emergency harvesting protocols for municipal, well, and residential home water storage, derived from CDC, FEMA, EPA, and Utah State University Extension standards.

---

## 1. Storage Container Engineering & Material Selection

### Approved Food-Grade Containers:
* Food-Grade High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE #2): Marked with the recycling triangle #2 and food-safety symbol (fork and cup). Chemical leaching resistant and impact durable.
* Standard 55-Gallon Blue Drums: The industry standard for residential bulk water storage. Blue pigment blocks light penetration, preventing algae photosynthesis.
* Stackable Water Bricks (3.5 Gallon) & Rigid Jerrycans (5 Gallon): Sized for transportability (water weighs 1 kg/L or 8.34 lbs/gal; 5 gallons weighs approx. 42 lbs / 19 kg).
* Intermediate Bulk Containers (IBC Totes - 275 / 330 Gallons): Must be certified food-grade virgin resin. Never use reconditioned chemical totes.

### Dangerous Containers to NEVER Use for Drinking Water:
* Discarded Plastic Milk Jugs: Made of thin, biodegradable low-density resin designed to break down rapidly. They become brittle and develop catastrophic leaks within 3 to 6 months. Milk protein residues cannot be fully sterilized, fueling rapid bacterial growth.
* Used Chemical or Detergent Drums: Residual industrial surfactants, petroleum derivatives, and pesticides penetrate the porous polymer matrix of plastic and leach back into drinking water indefinitely.

## 2. Container Sanitization Protocol (Before Initial Fill)

Prior to storing water, all containers (new or reused) must be biologically sterilized:
1. Wash interior surfaces with hot water and dishwashing detergent; rinse thoroughly.
2. Prepare Sanitizing Solution: Mix 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of unscented liquid household bleach (5% to 8.25% sodium hypochlorite) per 1 Liter (approx. 1 Quart) of clean water.
3. Swirl sanitizing solution inside the closed container ensuring contact with all internal walls, neck, and lid threads for at least 30 to 60 seconds.
4. Drain bleach solution and let air-dry or rinse with safe boiled/treated water before final fill.

## 3. Water Preservation & Rotation Schedules

### Standard Municipal Tap Water (6-Month Rotation):
* City water already contains residual chlorine. If stored in a sanitized food-grade container, it remains safe for 6 months.
* Rotation Rule: Dump, sanitize, and refill every 6 months.

### Extended 5-Year Storage Protocol (Water Preserver / Chlorine Dioxide):
* Adding an EPA-registered water preserver (stabilized chlorine dioxide - ClO2) eliminates the burden of semiannual rotation.
* Dosage: Commercial concentrate treats 55-gallon drums, maintaining guaranteed microbial safety for 5 full years without algae, biofilm, or bacterial contamination.

### Storage Environment Physics:
* NEVER Store Directly on Bare Concrete: Concrete leaches minerals, transfers extreme temperature differentials, and allows volatile chemical vapors (from garage floors) to pass through porous plastic barrels. Always store containers elevated on wooden pallets, 2x4 lumber, or thick cardboard.
* Chemical Isolation: Store barrels far away from gasoline cans, motor oil, paints, varnishes, and pesticides. Vapors permeate plastic walls over prolonged storage.
* UV Protection: Keep in dark basements, closets, or covered with heavy opaque tarps to prevent algae blooms.

## 4. Harvesting Hidden Residential Water Reserves (When Municipal Grid Fails)

When municipal pipes lose pressure, an average home contains 150 to 300 liters of clean potable water trapped in its internal plumbing:

### 1. The Water Heater / Boiler Tank (120 to 200 Liters / 30 to 50 Gallons)
* The largest single reserve of clean drinking water in most homes.
* Harvesting Procedure:
  1. Turn OFF the electric breaker or gas supply to the water heater (prevents heating elements from burning out when dry).
  2. Shut off the home's main water supply valve at the street (prevents contaminated backflow).
  3. Attach a garden hose to the drain valve at the bottom of the heater tank.
  4. Open a hot water faucet on the top floor of the house (breaks the vacuum and allows water to flow).
  5. Open the bottom drain valve and collect clean drinking water into buckets or pitchers.

### 2. Gravity-Draining Interior Pipes (20 to 40 Liters)
* Trapped water sits in vertical house pipes.
* Open the faucet at the highest point of the house, then open the lowest faucet (basement or garden hose bib) to collect gravity-drained water.

### 3. The Toilet Tank / Cistern (10 to 15 Liters)
* The gravity tank behind the toilet contains clean municipal water before it enters the bowl.
* Safe for boiling or purification (provided no chemical blue toilet bowl cleaner tablets have been added).
