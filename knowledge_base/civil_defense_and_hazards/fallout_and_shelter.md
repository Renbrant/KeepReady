# Knowledge Base: Civil Defense & Radiological Protection

## 1. The 7-10 Radiation Decay Rule (Oak Ridge National Laboratory)

Nuclear weapon fallout radioactivity decays predictably according to the 7-10 rule of thumb:
* For every 7-fold increase in time after detonation, the radiation dose rate decreases by a factor of 10.
* Detonation + 1 hour: 1,000 R/hr (Extreme Lethal Hazard)
* Detonation + 7 hours (7x): 100 R/hr (10-fold reduction)
* Detonation + 49 hours (approx. 2 days, 7x7): 10 R/hr (100-fold reduction)
* Detonation + 343 hours (approx. 2 weeks, 7x7x7): 1 R/hr (1,000-fold reduction)
* Practical Rule: Shelter-in-place is mandatory for a minimum of 48 hours to 14 days before considering outdoor transit.

## 2. Radiation Shielding Half-Thickness Values

The thickness of a material required to cut incoming gamma radiation intensity by exactly 50% (one half-value layer):

* Packed Soil / Earth: 15 cm (6 inches)
* Solid Concrete: 10 cm (4 inches)
* Water: 18 cm (7.2 inches)
* Solid Steel: 2.5 cm (1 inch)
* Solid Lead: 1.0 cm (0.4 inches)

Rule for Shelter Construction: 10 half-value layers (e.g., 100 cm of concrete or 150 cm of packed earth) reduces outside radiation by a factor of 1,000 (0.1% penetration).

## 3. The Nordic 7-Day Household Autonomy Standard (MSB Sweden)

Official civil defense baseline for civilian households during national crises or infrastructure loss:
* 7 days of non-perishable, ready-to-eat food per member.
* 21 liters of potable water stored per person (3 L/day).
* One battery-operated or hand-crank AM/FM radio for civil alert broadcasts.
* Warm clothing, sleeping bags, and one designated heated room per dwelling.
* Alternative cash reserve in small banknotes for commercial retail transactions during digital payment blackouts.
